# Executive Revenue Dashboard

## From KPI collection to management decision

An executive dashboard should help leadership understand performance, locate a material gap, and decide what to investigate next.

> Where is performance falling behind plan, and what should leadership investigate first?

This fictional example contains the same H1 revenue data in both versions.

## Before

![Before: a crowded sales dashboard uses eight KPI cards, a regional pie chart, monthly columns, and a margin gauge.](../../assets/case-studies/executive-revenue-dashboard/before.svg)

The page treats every metric as equally important. Exact cents compete with summary KPIs, regional share is separated from regional performance, and the gauge shows margin without making the target gap prominent. The reader must assemble the story.

## After

![After: an optimized executive revenue dashboard focuses on the gap to plan, trend, regional variance, and next decision.](../../assets/case-studies/executive-revenue-dashboard/after.svg)

**Finding:** H1 revenue is $12.4M, or 3.9% below plan. Gross margin is 1.6 points below target, and West has the largest regional shortfall.

The redesign establishes a reading sequence:

1. Read the finding.
2. Confirm the size and direction of the gap.
3. Examine the monthly trend against plan.
4. Locate the largest regional variance.
5. Take the stated follow-up action.

## What changed

| Decision | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Eight KPI cards to three | Keep the principal performance signals visible. |
| Exact dollars to rounded millions | Match precision to an executive monitoring task. |
| Pie chart to variance bars | Show performance against plan by region. |
| Standalone columns to actual-versus-plan lines | Make the widening monthly gap visible. |
| Gauge to direct margin variance | State the distance from target explicitly. |
| Generic title to supported finding | Give leadership the conclusion before the detail. |
| Add a decision panel | Connect the evidence to the next investigation. |
